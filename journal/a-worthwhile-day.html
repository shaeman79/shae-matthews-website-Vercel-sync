What would make tomorrow a worthwhile day?

A chosen direction. One feasible action. An honest account of what happened.

Liberated Man editorial · 5 minute read

You can have a clear vision for your life and still finish a Friday unsure whether anything important moved. The temptation is to demand a more ambitious plan. Another possibility is to ask a better question: what would count as a worthwhile day, and what would you actually notice if you lived one?

Three very different sources offer useful starting points. They do not establish a single method, and none validates a coaching product. Read together, they invite a distinction between the direction we choose, the conditions we live within, and the attention we bring to an ordinary action.

The OECD’s well-being work argues that economic output alone cannot describe how people are doing. Its approach considers wider economic, social and environmental outcomes, including who benefits and what can be sustained. This is a framework for public measurement and policy, not a personal scorecard or evidence that tracking your life improves it.

The editorial translation is modest: choose your measures with care. If your desired life includes friendship, creative work and enough space to recover, a list counting only completed work tasks will leave important things invisible. That does not make earning a living unimportant. It asks whether your definition of progress includes the life that earning is meant to support.

Plum Village’s practice guidance locates contemplative attention in everyday activities, including walking, eating and resting. It describes awareness of natural breathing rather than forcing the breath. This is Buddhist teaching and practice guidance; the page is not an outcome study, and its broader healing language should not be treated as a demonstrated medical effect.

Our interpretation is to leave some room in a plan for actually experiencing it. A walk does not have to become another scored achievement. You might simply notice your surroundings, if walking is available and comfortable for you. Another accessible ordinary activity can serve the same editorial purpose: a moment you choose to inhabit, without a requirement to report success.

UNESCO’s Futures of Education programme describes learning as a lifelong and shared undertaking. Its proposals include cooperation and connecting learning across different settings. These are institutional directions for education, not proof that a particular lesson format works. Their scale matters: educational opportunity cannot be reduced to an individual’s willingness to try harder.

For a personal experiment, that suggests asking where an idea meets the world. Reading about clearer communication and trying to state a request are different activities. If another person is involved, their willingness and response matter. They are a participant with their own aims, rather than an instrument for completing your growth exercise.

There is a tension here worth keeping. Measurement can clarify choices, but a ledger cannot contain a whole life. Attention can make an ordinary moment worth noticing, but it cannot create affordable housing or spare time. Learning can widen possibilities, but opportunity and support are uneven. A useful practice should help you see these distinctions instead of blaming yourself for every constraint.

Try it tomorrow

Here is a small proposal for tomorrow, offered as an editorial experiment rather than a validated intervention. Write one sentence about a direction you want: perhaps more room for friendship, or a less hurried start. Choose one action that fits your actual capacity. Name what could obstruct it and whether you need another person’s agreement, practical support, or a smaller version of the action.

At the end of the day, record what happened in plain language. What did you do? What was different from your expectation? What condition helped or got in the way? You can leave the result mixed. The point is to make the next choice better informed, without turning an imperfect day into a verdict on your worth.

That is enough for a first experiment: a chosen direction, a real action, and an honest account. Keep the plan small enough that there is still a life around it.
